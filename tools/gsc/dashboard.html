Is this normal for a 4-month-old site?

Yes. Across the whole web, 96.55% of pages get zero traffic from Google, and only 1.74% of new pages reach the first page of results within a year. Nearly three quarters of the pages sitting in Google's top 10 are over three years old.

So 2 visitors a week at 4 months is an ordinary starting point, not a broken site. The real verdict comes on the date below — not from any single week on this page.

What is holding us back

Google has only read 35 of our 235 pages (15%).

Google has to read a page before it can ever show it to anyone, so the other 200 pages might as well not exist yet. But this is not fixed by editing those pages: we checked, and on our own site neither length nor internal links makes any difference to whether Google reads a page (pages with 9+ internal links are indexed 0% of the time; pages with one link, 6%). Google is judging the whole site, and what changes that judgement is other websites linking to us — which is why the job below is emails, not editing.

How this all works

It always happens in this order, and you cannot skip a step:

1. We build a page. Instant — we control this.
2. Google reads it. Days to months. We push this along by submitting the 10 links above.
3. It starts showing up when someone searches. Weeks after that.
4. Someone clicks and visits. This is last, and slowest, because it only happens once we climb high enough in the results to be seen.

So early on, the first two numbers are the ones that move — and they are the two you can actually control. The last one is the reward for the other three.
